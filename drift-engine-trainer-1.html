How to use the deck

a three-step guide to building a drift sentence

Hit Deal a new hand and you'll get a card from each of the essential banks. Use the cards to construct a sentence — if you do it correctly, the sentence will create a trance, like the one shown at the bottom of this guide about a glass plate inside a microwave.

1

Set the scene with your sensory cards

Pick somewhere ordinary — a kitchen works great. Apply the three-descriptor rule: stack three sensory adjectives in front of your noun. Don't strain for poetry. Plain words are fine.

"I was at my parents' house and there was this clear smooth glass plate sitting inside a heavy dim microwave."

clear smooth glass is the three-descriptor rule applied to the plate. heavy dim is a second sensory pair anchoring the microwave itself.

Good three-descriptor examples to study:

Dark oak tree  ·  Thick hollow water  ·  Scaly green alligator

2

Add motion with your kinetic phrase and adverb cards

Find an object in the scene and let it start moving on its own. Your kinetic phrase card already has the verb built in — just place it. Then layer in your adverb cards to color the motion. A second continuous verb running in parallel locks the loop.

"...and it kept slowly turning quietly while there was this warm lightbulb steadily flickering rather brightly inside of this dense microwave"

kept turning is the kinetic phrase holding the motion in perpetual present. while is a subordinating conjunction — the listener never lands. flickering keeps a second loop running in parallel.

Examples of stacked motion:

Kept moving  ·  Just continued drifting  ·  Was steadily expanding

3

Layer in drift linguistics

Now slip in your soft qualifier, nominalization, and unspecified verb cards — indirectly. Never name a feeling. Let the nominalization arrive on its own through a phrase like "with an understanding that…" or "there was this awareness…"

"...which was just jammed completely open, so with an understanding that I'd have to close it I really tried to close this microwave but it was stuck open."

with an understanding that… frames the nominalization indirectly — the noun walks through the door the soft qualifier opens. tried to close is an unspecified verb pattern that lets the listener fill in their own picture.

Highly recommended: combine a three-word adjective–adverb sequence in every sentence to anchor the drift.

All three layers together — the complete sentence
"I was at my parents' house and there was this clear smooth glass plate sitting inside a heavy dim microwave, and it kept slowly turning quietly while there was this warm lightbulb steadily flickering rather brightly inside of this dense microwave which was just jammed completely open, so with an understanding that I'd have to close it I really tried to close this microwave but it was stuck open."

Deal a fresh hand, pick an ordinary place, and build your own three-step sentence.
Press the spacebar any time to draw again. The more loops you run, the deeper the practice settles.
